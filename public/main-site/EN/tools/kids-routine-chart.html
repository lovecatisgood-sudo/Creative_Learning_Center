    How to make a routine chart children can follow

    Start with the few steps that matter most. A visual routine is easier to use when each card describes one concrete action rather than a long instruction. You can always add detail after the sequence feels familiar.

    Use the starter routine as a draft, then change it to match your actual home. Times are optional. For younger children, a clear order can be more useful than an exact clock time.

    Put the printed chart where the routine happens: beside the school-bag station, near the bathroom, or at the child’s eye level in the bedroom.

    Example: a simple family routine

    Snack → homework → creative time → dinner → shower → reading. Keep only the steps your family can repeat most days.

    Why a visual routine can be practical at home

    A visual chart does not need to be complicated. Its job is to make the next step easy to see. Children can point to the card, check off a finished step, and gradually take more ownership of familiar routines.

    Keep the chart flexible. School days, holidays, tired days and family events will not all look the same. You can return to this builder whenever the routine needs to change.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is this routine chart really free?

    Yes. The builder works in the browser and does not require an account.

    Can I print the chart on A4 paper?

    Yes. Use Print / Save PDF. The print layout hides the website navigation and formats the routine as a clean A4-friendly chart.

    Can I use it for more than one child?

    Yes. Save or download a chart for one child, then change the name and routine for another. Browser storage is local to the device.
