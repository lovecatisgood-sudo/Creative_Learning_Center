Why the result is a starting point

Accuracy comes from calculation + measurement

The strongest version of this calculator does not hide disagreement between references. It shows the equation, the cross-check, and when your own stable intake is better evidence for your individual cat.

RER / MER cross-check

RER = 70 × BWkg0.75

AAHA and Merck use RER = 70 × kg^0.75, then multiply by life-stage factors. The calculator displays this as a cross-check.

Cat-specific metabolic weight

Adult cat MER ≈ factor × BWkg0.67

FEDIAF 2025 uses kg^0.67 for cats and gives separate adult, kitten, gestation and lactation references. That is the primary population model here.

BCS-derived ideal weight

Ideal BW ≈ current BW ÷ (1 + 0.1 × [BCS − 5])

For BCS 6–9, AAHA relates each point above 5/9 to roughly 10% excess body weight. The estimate is less reliable at extreme obesity.

Real-world personalization

If an ideal-BCS cat has been eating a measured number of calories, weight is stable for at least 14 days, and there are at least 3 weigh-ins, that observed intake is used as the maintenance starting point.

How to get the most accurate result in practice

01

Calculate

Use weight, life stage, BCS, neuter status and activity.

02

Weigh the food

Prefer grams and kcal/kg instead of a loosely filled measuring cup.

03

Re-weigh the cat

Track body weight and BCS on the same scale and similar conditions.

04

Adjust to response

Use the measured trend, not blind faith in the first number.

Primary and authoritative references used

FEDIAF 2025 Nutritional Guidelines

Cat-specific kg^0.67 energy references for adult cats, kittens, gestation and lactation.

WSAVA Global Nutrition Guidelines

Body condition and muscle-condition assessment resources.

Merck Veterinary Manual

Independent RER/MER table and reminder that calculated intake is a starting point.

AAHA 2014 Weight Management Guidelines

Ideal-weight estimation, 80% RER method and 0.5–2%/week feline monitoring range.

Cornell Feline Health Center — Anorexia

Safety reference for prompt veterinary assessment when a mature cat stops eating; Cornell notes health effects can become serious within about 24 hours.

Useful public comparison tool and safety messaging for ideal-BCS cats.
